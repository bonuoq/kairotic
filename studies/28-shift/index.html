The samples of a buffer are too big for the history: they stay in this browser (IndexedDB) under the hash of the samples, and the work keeps only their description. A buffer made by shift also keeps its recipe: the source it came from (by hash) and the factors, formants and analysis that made it. Forget its sound and remake it: the same recipe gives the same samples, so the hash comes back. Factors are written as ratios (3/2, 1.25), as semitones with a sign (+7, -12, +3.86, and 0 for the unison) or in cents (386c). Several factors make a chord; a progression changes them over time, one line per step, seconds: factors. Pitch shifting by stftPitchShift 2.0 by Jürgen Hock (MIT), built for the web with wasi-sdk. The library warns that formant preservation is weaker with several factors and small ones.
