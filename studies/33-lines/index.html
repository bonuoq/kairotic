What this study tries

  
A work becomes a project: several lines, each a whole history tree of its own (branches, front), shown as tabs. patch name in any line is the line name as a sub-patch, its inlet and outlet boxes as ports. By default it follows the line — it is the line at its front, its newest point, so it changes as that line advances, like an abstraction of Max or Pd; pinned, it stays at one point of the line's history. Lines nest, and a cycle is refused. An embedded patch can be extracted to a line of its own, with the history it had inside its box, and a reference inlined back into its box. Going back in a tab and editing makes a branch, and that branch is the line's newest point: the boxes that follow it follow it. Renaming a line rewrites its users, and older points still find it by its old name. A release takes the main line (★) and every line it reaches, each at the point it uses, into a new project where every reference is pinned; a line used at two points becomes two lines. A line can be exported with its history and imported in another project. Tutorials and help patches can be projects too: one tab per view of a topic, not a sequence of steps (see the example Help · patch). The project is kept in this browser.
