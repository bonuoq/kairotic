Study 74. The singer (ἀοιδός) reads the text before singing it: its syllables (a diphthong one nucleus unless a diaeresis parts it; between two vowels one consonant begins the next syllable, of two the first closes the one before, a stop and a liquid may begin the next together), their quantity — long by nature (η, ω, a diphthong, a circumflex), short (ε, ο), doubtful (α, ι, υ: the laws of the accent settle some, the metre the rest), long by position before two consonants, and a long vowel before a vowel shortened (epic correption) — and their accent. The line is scanned in its metre (the hexameter's dactyls and spondees, with synizesis where the line needs it: Πηληϊάδεω), and the scansion is the time: a short syllable a beat, a long one two, held over the beat (Study 41's tie), the line's last syllable to the end of its foot. The melody is a voice of @kairotic/rules (solve(), counted exactly): its hard rules are every line's (the mode's ambitus, the start, Fux's melodic intervals, a leap turned, the end on the final by step), its costs the accent's — Greek's accent was a pitch, and the music that survives follows it: the voice rises to an acute, the accented syllable is the word's highest, a circumflex falls on its long syllable (two notes), after the accent the voice falls, after a grave it does not fall until the next accent — weighed with soft.mjs's melodic costs, the same as every engine's. Strict makes the peak and the circumflex hard rules. Imitable? tries the melody as the dux of cac imitation: a melody sung alone almost never has a strict canon, but often imitation near it. In canon the dux is written together with its comes (canon.mjs's rules, canon-2): the singer chooses among the futuribles of the comes, as #192 first thought it — the dux sings the text, a new syllable a new note, the comes answers a foot later. Odyssey 1.1 in strict canon at the octave below has 4285 such canons; Seikilos's song, 6522. Not yet: Seikilos's own melody as a test of the accent rules, the poem's text under the notes in the MEI, the harmonization as a chorale.
