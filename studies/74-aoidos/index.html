About

Study 74. The singer (ἀοιδός) reads the text before singing it: its syllables (a diphthong one nucleus unless a diaeresis parts it; between two vowels one consonant begins the next syllable, of two the first closes the one before, a stop and a liquid may begin the next together), their quantity — long by nature (η, ω, a diphthong, a circumflex), short (ε, ο), doubtful (α, ι, υ: the laws of the accent settle some, the metre the rest), long by position before two consonants, and a long vowel before a vowel shortened (epic correption) — and their accent. The line is scanned in its metre (the hexameter's dactyls and spondees, with synizesis where the line needs it: Πηληϊάδεω), and the scansion is the time: a short syllable a beat, a long one two, held over the beat (Study 41's tie), the line's last syllable to the end of its foot — in Seikilos's metre every line twelve chronoi, the last syllable and then the longs from the end lengthened, which is the stele's rhythm syllable by syllable. The melody is written a beat at a time: a syllable may be sung on up to three notes (a melisma, by step or a 3rd, as Seikilos sings φαί on three). The melody is a voice of @kairotic/rules (solve(), counted exactly): its hard rules are every line's (the mode's ambitus, the start, Fux's melodic intervals, a leap turned, the end on the final by step), its costs the accent's — Greek's accent was a pitch, and the music that survives follows it: the voice rises to an acute, the accented syllable holds the word's highest note, a circumflex falls within its syllable (on one note, half the cost), after the accent the voice falls, after a grave it does not fall until the next accent — weighed with soft.mjs's melodic costs, the same as every engine's. Strict makes the peak and the circumflex hard rules. Imitable? tries the melody as the dux of cac imitation (▶ hears it with the comes the engine chose): a melody sung alone almost never has a strict canon, but often imitation near it. In canon the dux is written together with its comes (canon.mjs's rules, canon-2): the singer chooses among the futuribles of the comes, as #192 first thought it — the dux sings the text, a new syllable a new note, the comes answers a foot later. Odyssey 1.1 in strict canon at the octave below has 4285 such canons; Seikilos's song, 6522. The stele's melody (Pöhlmann–West, DAGM 23) keeps every hard rule of the engine: it is one of the melodies aoidos may sing; the engine would choose its note at 21 of its 48 beats. With an aulos, the double pipe that went with the voice: in heterophony it plays the melody at once with its own deviations, within a 3rd (the two counted together under the counterpoint's rules, as Plato's Laws describe the lyre doing what they would forbid the young); as a drone, the final held an octave below; as an ison that follows the song, each line held on the note it ends on. Pitch: a church mode in 12-TET, or a Greek tonos (greek.mjs) — the Immutable System of two octaves and its conjunct tetrachord in any of the fifteen tonoi, diatonic, chromatic or enharmonic, in Aristoxenus's tuning or Archytas's ratios, heard in cents; its notes counted by their place (the chromatic lichanos a step below the mese, a minor third away), its moves judged by their size, the final any of its notes (Seikilos's is the Iastian's lichanos meson, e). There the canon, the heterophony and the examiner follow the Greek counterpoint: the only concords the unison, the 4th, the 5th and the octave, on every strong beat; a discord only passing, by step, on a weak one; parallel concords allowed (the aulos with the voice, the octaves of the magadis). The aulos in heterophony keeps within a 4th of the voice and near it by choice: mostly in unison, its deviations the exception. The Greek style is learned from Mesomedes's hymns (DAGM 24, 25, 27) and the two Delphic paeans, Athenaios's and Limenios's (DAGM 20, 21), read in their tonoi (corpus.mjs, style.mjs): the weights under which what they wrote is most likely — leaps dear, a note struck again free (a recitation tone, up to eight times), a melisma dear, no turn asked after a 4th or a 5th. Seikilos, which it has not seen, becomes more likely: the cheapest note at 26 of its 48 beats. Not yet: the harmonization as a chorale.
