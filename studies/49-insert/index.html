About

Study 49. Point 2 of the plan after the discussion on Faust and rings: a Faust DSP in the feedback of a ring of sound, inside the same node. The page compiles the code (faustwasm) and sends the WebAssembly module to the worklet that holds the span; there it runs one sample at a time, between what a reader sends (times feed) and what the writer writes. So the loop is exact: what the reader meets comes back d later, to the sample, even when d is shorter than a block — a plucked string is a ring whose d is its period and whose insert is its damping. With outside the feedback waits one block (128 samples), as it does when the loop goes through other boxes of a patch (reader~ → faust~ → writer~): the string is out of tune, the comb rings at another pitch. Values are transformed in the loop, not by the heads (decision 4): the insert is that transformation.

What it decides

  • An insert runs one sample at a time (compute(1)): exact at any d, at the cost of a call per sample.
  • It runs on every sample, even in silence, so a filter's state goes on.
  • Every input of the DSP gets the feedback; its first output is written.
