About

The comes is the dux transformed, and the transformation is data: a canon (the dux later, shifted), an inversion (the dux upside down about a pitch: contrary motion), an augmentation (the dux twice as slow), a crab (the dux backwards, both voices at once) and a table canon (backwards and upside down: the one score read by a second player from across the table, as in the duet for two violins attributed to Mozart). The first three are written from the beginning, a note a beat. A crab or a table canon cannot be: the comes at the start is the dux at the end. They are written from both ends to the middle — each step writes a note at the start and one at the end (choose the one at the start, then the one at the end) — and the futuribles are the ways to close the work in the middle. A crab must be invertible (each vertical comes back with its voices exchanged) and its line good both ways. Turn the page: in a crab the score mirrored left to right, in a table canon turned upside down, is what the other voice plays — a musical ambigram. On the white keys D is the axis that keeps every interval as it is: the dorian mode is its own mirror. The rules and the systems are Study 42's (the counts of a canon are the same); the futures are counted exactly. The work is kept in this browser.
