    About discussion #321

    An analysis is the perpendicular of the history: the history runs along the time of composing; an analysis goes down, at one point of it, through the levels of what a phrase prolongs. Here a phrase is reduced a step at a time, each step one of the proto-voice model's operations read backwards (Finkensiep & Rohrmeier, ISMIR 2021; DCMLab/protovoices-haskell): an unsplit takes away a slice whose notes are all ornaments of the notes around them (a note held, struck again, a neighbour, a passing tone, an incomplete neighbour), an unspread hears two slices as one sonority. Read forwards, the steps are the derivation that composes the phrase from its frame.

    Which step is taken is one policy: the ornaments' own cost, GTTM's metrical weight (a slice on a strong unit is dearer to take away) and its cadential retention (the frame keeps the first slice and the cadence), dissonance (a dissonant slice goes first), the soft rules of every engine (soft.mjs: how the melody left moves), and the hard rules of every phrase (partimento.mjs, through Study 58's check): a step that makes the level refuse itself around it is taken only when no other is left. A level below the surface is checked note against note, a slice a unit.

    • Varying from a level keeps the melody's notes that survive there (on their units, held as long) and writes the others again with the engine of Studies 56–60; the deeper the level, the less is kept. From the surface nothing changes; from the frame almost everything does.
    • An analysis is an interpretation: warmer readings take other steps, as an analyst might.
    • What comes next (#321): the levels drawn as a score with the proto-voice edges, exchange with DCMLab's annotation tool, the analysis kept in the work, and the futuribles taking its open obligations.
