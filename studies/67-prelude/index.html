    About

    Study 66 describes BWV 846 by one figure and the five pitches each bar gives it, and reads the bars it does not cover from their notes (the plagal close over the tonic pedal, C7 – F/C – G7 – C). What is left is a progression of five voices: each bar's pitches, low to high, a voice each; a voice that keeps its pitch from one bar to the next holds its note (the G of the dominant pedal is one note across eight bars). A progression of voices is what Study 61 reduces: the same grammar (unsplit, unspread, unsuspend — the proto-voices of Finkensiep and Rohrmeier read backwards), GTTM's metre, the economy of the derivation, a chord a unit, the first and the last chord kept and the one where the bass arrives for the cadence. With the tonal functions as its judge (functions.mjs: no level may go back from a dominant, every applied dominant resolved), its frame is C – Am/C – D7 – C♯°7 – Dm/F – G7 – C – Fmaj7 – F♯°7 – G7 – C/G – C7 – F/C – C: the applied dominants kept with what they lead to — C7, V of ④, with its F — and the plagal close over the tonic pedal.

    Each level is said in bits with Study 66's coder (bits.mjs, a lexicon for each context, an escape for a word never heard): each chord's length and every voice's step from its pitch before — the surface said exactly as Study 66 says its figure's pitches, so the numbers are the same measure. The frame says the harmony in about half of the surface's bits: as a lossy description, a level is a compression. To come back to the surface exactly, the derivation must be said too, and it costs more than it saves: the five voices already move by small steps, cheap words. The reduction explains the harmony more than it compresses it.

    Read forwards, the grammar composes (#321: one grammar, both ways). From a level, its chords are kept where they are and every bar between them is written again, voice by voice — held, a step, a third — to a major or minor triad, the voices not crossing, the steps already said preferred; then Study 66's figure is put on every chord: a new prelude in Bach's manner, the deeper the level the less of his. Each new chord keeps, as far as it can, the tension Bach's has at that bar (Lerdahl's distance from the tonic, tension.mjs): the curve under the chords shows both. Each span between two chords kept is an exact bridge over chords: triads or sevenths (a 7th going down by step), the five voices in order (none lost on another's pitch), no upper voice leaping more than a 4th nor the bass more than a 5th — into the chord kept next too —, no parallel 5ths or octaves between any two voices; where no new way keeps them, Bach's chords stay. Its key is C major's scale. Both the new prelude and any level can be exported as MEI for the app.
