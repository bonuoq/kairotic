About discussion #342

A note is legal if a legal derivation makes it. The frame is second and fourth species over the cantus firmus — consonance on the strong half, a passing note on the weak one, suspensions prepared and resolved — drawn by a bridge (Study 63) over the kernel every phrase answers to. The rhythm comes next, a cell a half bar — a half note, two quarters, a dotted quarter and an eighth, a quarter and two eighths (eighths in pairs, on the weak quarter, as Fux writes them) — drawn by a bridge over the bars with GTTM's preferences: the downbeat's note the longest (MPR 5), a bar like the one before it once but not a third time (parallelism, MPR 1), the bar's second half the busier, and an arc of activity over the phrase. Each half bar of the frame, from its note to the next, is then elaborated on its cell by a figure: a shape relative to the two notes it joins, and the places where it may be dissonant — the dissonance it brings is justified by the frame it ornaments — chosen with the melody's coherence on the surface (the soft rules of every engine on the florid line itself, a direction that goes on, a figure in sequence). A suspension of the frame may be prepared with a neighbour, or resolved later: early, in an eighth, or through a third below it (an ornamented resolution).

  • The figures: a passing note (transitus), a neighbour, in quarters or eighths, a run (tirata), a turn (circulatio), a skip filled in eighths, an anticipation (anticipatio), an escape tone, a skip to a note of the chord, a cambiata, and the suspension's own.
  • Reduced by Study 61's grammar on the eighths' grid (GTTM's weights inside the bar), the frame's downbeats come back: the derivation read both ways.
