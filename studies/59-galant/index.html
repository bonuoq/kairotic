What it decides

The schemata of the Galant style (about 1720–1770, and long after), each a bass, its chords and the motion of the upper voices, realized in three parts under the rules of Studies 51, 54 and 57 (@kairotic/cac's partimento.mjs); the book's prototypes are among the realizations, outlined.

  • The leaping Romanesca (Pachelbel): a 4th down and a 2nd up, falling thirds, complete and incomplete 5/3 chords by turns; in minor on the natural scale, its ⑤ passed in the sequence. The Monte Romanesca: a 5th up and a 4th down, rising by step, ③–⑦ (in minor ②–⑥) left out for its diminished 5/3.
  • The Quiescenza: a pedal on ①, the bass held under chords of its own — (5)/3, ♭7/3, 6/4, 7/4, (5)/3 —, dissonances that come and go by step; its chromatic tone (♭⑦ in major, ♯③ in minor) read as a key of its own, toward ④, and back.
  • The Prinner: ④ ③ ② ① under 6̂ 5̂ 4̂ 3̂ in 10ths, a 5/3 on ④ (not the RO's 4/2), a 7–6 on ②; the modulating Prinner ① ⑦ ⑥ ⑤ is the ④ ③ ② ① of the dominant.
  • The Fenaroli: the bass circling the tonic, ① ② ③ ⑦; a canon: one upper voice sings the bass again two units later, the other holds 5̂ (a pedal above). The Folia: minor only, its natural ⑦ leaping to ③, the upper voices in parallel 6ths.
  • Phrases join them as the book's exercises do: Handel's Chaconne (a Monte Romanesca, then a Galant cadence), a leaping Romanesca answered by a Prinner.
