About

A voice made of parts that can be read, after formant synthesis (Klatt's cascade and parallel synthesizer, 1980; Rodet's CHANT) rather than a neural model whose voice is a black box (discussion #467). The line comes with its syllables — Seikilos's song as the stele has it, or an MEI that Study 74 (aoidos) wrote, with its text under the notes. Each syllable is read into phonemes in a pronunciation chosen among reconstructions (Attic, after Allen's Vox Graeca; or the Koine of the stele's own century). Each phoneme has its targets in the table — formants, voicing, aspiration, frication —, editable here as EDN. The curves go from target to target: onset consonants just before the beat, a stop as closure, burst and aspiration, the formants gliding between neighbours (that glide is most of what makes a consonant heard), the first formant raised over a high pitch. Two faust~ follow the same curves: the voice (a glottal source through four formant resonators) and the noise (aspiration through the same four, frication and bursts at their own place). The sound is clearly synthetic — nearer CHANT than a singer — but every part of it can be studied and changed. Nasals still lack their antiformant.
