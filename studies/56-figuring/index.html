What it decides

The partimento turned round: the bass comes without figures and the melody is improvised over it, under the two-part rules of Studies 51 and 54 (@kairotic/cac's partimento.mjs) — a note played is taken as it is where the rules and the rest of the phrase allow it, else the nearest one that can be played, and why.

  • The figures are found, not given. Each note of the melody is an interval over the bass; the chords it can belong to are those whose figures hold it, the likeliest first: the rule of the octave (the scale in the bass figured as the partimento schools taught it: ① 5/3, ② 6/3, ③ 6/3, ④ 6 going up and 4/2 going down, ⑤ 5/3, ⑥ 6/3, ⑦ 6/3), then what the melody's own dissonance names — a 7th held over the bass a 7/3, a 4th a 5/4, a 2nd over the bass gone down under it a 4/2 —, and a dominant ⑤ a 5/3 or a 7/3. A dissonance on a weak unit passes: it has no chord of its own.
  • The schemata are recognized as the two lines make them: parallel 3rds or 6ths, the 3–6 progression, chains of 7–6 or 2–3, and the cadences of chapter 3 — imperfect 3–3, perfect 6–8, compound 7–6–8 on ②–①; on ⑤–① perfect, imperfect or with a 4–3.
  • The cadence is counted ahead: the ways the melody so far leaves to make each kind of cadence the ending allows, and one of them written by the engine.
  • A middle voice is written from the figures under the three-part rules of Study 54 — no 3rd doubled nor the leading tone, no parallel 5ths or octaves between the upper voices, the 6/4 a double appoggiatura —, and the cadence a cadence: its last chord a 5/3 (never a 6), and from the dominant the leading tone up to the tonic, the 7th (④) down to ③, in either voice. Where none can be written it says where, and why: a melody that doubles the leading tone of the bass, or leaps where the voice under it cannot follow.
  • Writing a voice that allows a third, the melody and its figures are chosen in three parts: a note only where a middle voice can still be written under it and the melody finished over it, and at each unit the likeliest chord such a middle voice can make with the chords before it — so a middle voice can always be written from the figures. The ways on are then the three-part realizations: the melody and a middle voice.
  • The lines move as counterpoint asks, in some measure (line): moderate by default, a leap of a 5th or more turning back by step; strict, leaps up to a 5th or the octave, turned back by step from a 4th, never two the same way; free, the two-part rules alone. The middle voice, an inner voice, is one step stricter. Among what is allowed the engine still prefers steps and a leap compensated (the soft rules of Studies 38–42).
  • The phrase ends with a cadence, perfect or imperfect where the bass closes on ① (②–① or ⑤–①); a deceptive one (the last ① becomes the ⑥ above ⑤) or a half one (the phrase ends on ⑤) only when chosen.
