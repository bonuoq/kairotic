What it decides

Four parts over a given bass under chapter 6's rules (@kairotic/rules, the style chorale-4), on the basses the Rule of the Octave does not cover: leaping, sequential, tied. Each phrase is a prototype of the book's; the counts are exact.

  • The diminished 7th family — 7/5/3 on ⑦♯, 6/5/3 on ②, 6/4/3 on ④, 6/4/2 on ⑥ — is, as the dominant's, a family of semi-consonances: free to enter, its diminished 7th going down a half step. In major it needs the lowered ⑥ (molldur). It shares three tones with the dominant's 7th chord, and may stand in its place (the Meyer).
  • The circle of fifths: Fifth Down Fourth Up — 7th chords complete and incomplete by turns, each 7th prepared and resolved, or 5/3s —, Third Down Second Up (6/5–5/3; in minor 6/3–5/3) and the Tied Bass (6/4/2 over the tied note, 6/3) are one family, by voice exchange and invertible counterpoint. In a sequence the diminished triad is taken for the pattern's sake, and minor keeps its natural scale until the cadence.
  • Descending Thirds: a 5/3 on every bass note, each sharing two tones with the one before; in minor the Neapolitan ②♭ takes the place of the diminished triad, and the diminished 7th on ⑦♯ follows it.
  • The modulating Folia: ⑥–⑤–① in keys a third apart, minor and major by turns — the leaping Romanesca backwards.
