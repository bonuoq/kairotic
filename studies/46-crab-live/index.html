    About
    
  
    Study 46. In a crab canon the other voice is your line backwards: at beat t it plays your note n−t. Study 43 could not write it from the beginning and wrote it from both ends to the middle; here that order is played, live. In the first half you choose your note among the ones that can still close the crab (a note that cannot is taken as the nearest that can), and the engine answers at the same beat — with the end of your own line, chosen among the notes with a future: you see it written ahead on the score. At the middle the whole crab is written, and the rest of your line comes out as a score: the engine's answers, backwards. In the second half you read it, and the engine plays your first half backwards — each voice plays the other's past. By turns a note in is a beat; on a pulse the beat goes on, and if you played nothing the engine plays your note for you. A table canon is the same, upside down too. In 3/2 the crab still ends on a strong beat, so its strong beats mirror strong beats and only the 2nd and 3rd of each bar change places; with an even number of beats its middle is a pair, not a note.
