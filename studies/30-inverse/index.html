The descriptor distance is measured on standardised descriptors (level and Hz on log scales), per number; the patch distance is the mean difference of the parameters in [0, 1]. Both are compared with a patch picked at random from the bank. Where descriptors come much closer than a random pick but the patch does not, the descriptors do not tell those parameters apart: that is the ill-posed part of the problem, and why a model should be judged by how it sounds (descriptor distance), not by whether it guesses the numbers.






  
What comes next

  
This is the first step of the discussion on inverse synthesis: a reproducible bank (the synth is plain JavaScript, so the same seed gives the same samples in the page, a worker or Node: kairotic/generate.mjs makes large banks as NDJSON) and a baseline any model has to beat. Next: a small network trained on the bank (descriptor frames → parameters) whose guess is then refined by a search that listens (CMA-ES on the descriptor distance, rendering the real patch); the synth as a faust~ box so that a patch found is a recipe a work can keep; and, further on, patches whose structure is also found, written in Faust. In a work, the target need not be a sound: a curve of brightness or noisiness drawn over time is already a list of descriptors.
