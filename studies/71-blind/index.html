About

Each trial is one chorale phrase twice: as Bach harmonized it, and as a bridge — Study 63's outer voices drawn from the phrase's first chord to its last, the same length, the alto and the tenor then written by @kairotic/rules' fromOuter in the style bach-4. Both are played alike: a chord a quarter, a note tied where it is held, the last chord a fermata. Answer which one is Bach, then see what the two were. The bridges come in five variants: all (the tonal functions, the tension following Bach's mean profile, the economy of the voices' motion), each of those layers off (no-functions, no-tension, no-economy), and arch (all on, the tension following a generic arch instead). Where Bach is found no more often than chance (50%), the bridges pass; a layer whose absence makes him easier to find is one listeners hear. The deck (200 trials, drawn once) is the same for everyone: export your answers and pool them with others' by importing theirs. The deck also counts the bridges bach-4 could not fill — no inner voices by its rules over those outer voices — by variant. The phrases are from Craig Sapp's edition of the chorales (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
