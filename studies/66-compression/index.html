About
A piece is a stream of notes, each with its pitch and its rhythm; written out, a note costs
      7 bits of pitch and the bits of its rhythm. A figure is a pattern of a few pitches with its rhythm, used over other pitches
      (Bach's C major prelude: one figure over 32 chords); a motif is the same intervals from another pitch; a repeat says the
      same again. Each rule is paid once, each use only for what changes; the rules that save most are taken first (three orders are
      tried, the shortest kept), and the description is always written out again and compared note by note: it is lossless.
      What compresses well is figuration over harmony — what is left is the chords. Where no figure is (a cadence written out, the final chord, a line) the bar is read from its notes: the sounding pitches weighed by how long they sound, an anticipation of the next chord and the lightest passing notes left out — the harmony does not stop where the pattern does. The keyboard preludes compress to a third,
      the cello's to a little more than half: a line that is a melody says more. The scores are the Mutopia Project's public-domain
      typesets (BWV 846, 999 in its guitar edition in D minor, 1007, 1009), read by a small reader of their LilyPond.
      Heard, the same rules cost less: which rule comes is a word of a lexicon that learns as it goes (a rule used again costs
      almost nothing; the first time, an escape and its number), and a figure's pitches are told as each voice's step from the same
      voice of its last use — holding or moving by a step, the cheap words of voice leading (@kairotic/rules bits.mjs; discussion
      #399). The C major prelude comes to under a sixth. As a tree, the description is kairotic.mt's own: the rules in
      :defs, a figure used with :pitches — or, as voices, with :voices, each voice's step from its use before —, a motif with :transpose.
