What it decides

The bass is a phrase as the book's exercises build it — a schema, then a cadence — and two upper voices are written over it. Each is held to Study 51's rules against the bass; between them the rules of upper voices: a 4th is a consonance there, no parallel 5ths or octaves, no crossing, within an octave, a 2nd or a 7th only where one of them is the dissonance against the bass. The figures of a chord say which intervals sound above the bass, not in which voice; a 5/3 may leave out its 5th — the book's (5)/3 —, never its 3rd, and no two voices sound the same note a semitone apart (a false relation).

  • The chains of chapter 2 are the rules at work. In the 7–6 Fauxbourdon the 7th is held from the 6th before it; in the Fifth Down Fourth Up the 7th passes from one upper voice to the other, the 3rd of each chord held into the 7th of the next; in the Tied Bass the bass holds its note under a 4/2 and resolves down to a 6/3.
  • The cadences of chapter 3 are schemata too: simple, compound (a 6/4 or a 5/4 whose 4th is held over from the chord before), Galant (on ⑤ a 6/4 of accented passing tones, the only dissonance not prepared), deceptive, half.
  • Three voices, three notes of a chord: its 3rd is not doubled, nor the leading tone. In minor the chord on the dominant is major — ⑦ raised over a ⑤ that goes to ① (or ⑥) —, while a ⑤ passed in a sequence keeps the natural minor, as the book's 7–6 Fauxbourdon does; a raised ⑥ goes on up to the raised ⑦, toward the tonic.
  • A 6/4 over a held bass is a double appoggiatura: it resolves to the 5/3 with each voice down a step, the 6th to the 5th and the 4th to the 3rd in the same voice. On ④ the Galant cadence has the book's 6/(3), a sixth chord with its bass doubled.
  • A 7th may put off its resolution by one chord: the 7th of ② held into the 4th of the 6/4 or 5/4 over ⑤ (ii7 → V), and resolved down from there. So the Fifth Down Fourth Up closes with a compound or a deceptive cadence too; not with the Galant or the half one, whose ④ has no note for the 7th to go to.
  • The 6/3 chords with the 4th inverted into a 5th are parallel 5ths between the upper voices (ex. 2.6b), and refused.
