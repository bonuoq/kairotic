    About discussion #296
    
  
    An incipit is a phrase given — a bass and a melody over it, as a partimento's exercise begins — and taken on phrase by phrase. At a point of the piece Claude offers a few continuations, each a whole phrase with its plan named by IJzerman's schemata and cadences. Each is checked by the rules of two-part writing of Studies 51, 54 and 56 (@kairotic/cac's partimento.mjs), joined to the phrase before it so the join is judged too; a phrase that fails goes back to Claude with its errors (three tries in all), and the soft rules of Studies 38–42 rank the ones that pass. You choose which go on — one, several, none — and only those are taken further. Claude brings the idea; the rules bring the craft.
    
  
      
    
    • A step is large: a phrase at least (four bass notes or more), not a note.
      • 
    
    • Phrases may modulate, as Study 57 does (IJzerman, chapter 4): each bass note in a key of its own, read there — its leading tone, its dominant, the cadence it closes —, the augmented 4th and the diminished 5th as semi-consonances, and the Rule of the Octave's pentachords and tetrachords as the ways from one key to another. Ask for it (“to the dominant by an ascending tetrachord”), and the key reached is named over the score.
      • 
    
    • The phrases make a history with branches, kept in this browser. Any chosen point can be taken on again, by Claude or by hand (the engine writes a melody for a bass you give).
