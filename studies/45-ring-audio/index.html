The read head when the delay changes

tape
glides to the new delay at rate seconds a second: d growing lowers the pitch (rate 0.5, an octave down while it moves), shrinking raises it; above 1 the head goes backwards and the sound is reversed.
seek
jumps there and crossfades from where it was: no change of pitch, no click — a cut in the tape.
both
a small change glides, one larger than jump over is a seek: the slider dragged slowly sings, a click on it jumps (Study 44's rule).
freeze
stops writing: the write head still goes round, so the read head loops what the ring keeps — a looper; move d and the loop is read from elsewhere.

What it decides

  • Which head the ring~ box has by default (both?), and the jump and the crossfade it takes.
  • Whether ring and ring~ are one family: the same arguments (ring~ 8 both), the same :clear and :freeze, the delay on the right inlet — a number, or a signal (a phasor~ sweeping it: a tape with a hand on it).
  • Several read heads on one ring (a multi-tap delay: ring~ 8 0.25 0.5 0.75, an outlet each), and a reader~ reading it as it reads a sample.
  • Cubic or linear by default: cubic is cleaner when the head glides, linear is cheaper.
  • In the app: this worklet as the box, or Faust (de.fdelay) inside a faust~.

About

Study 45. Study 44's ring for sound. A ring~ keeps the last seconds of what it hears in a circular buffer: the write head goes round it at the speed of time, and the read head reads d seconds behind. With messages the ring sent what its head crossed; with sound the head reads at every sample, so the question is how it moves when d changes. A tape glides and changes the pitch while it moves — and goes backwards when d grows faster than time; a seek jumps, crossfading; both glides the small changes and jumps the large ones. Freeze stops writing and the ring loops; feedback writes what it reads back in (echoes). Press Start: a phrase loops into it; play the keys over it, or choose the microphone (from Kairotic Pages: a page published on claude.ai cannot ask for it).
