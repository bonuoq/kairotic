About
An open form is a page of fragments and the rules of their succession: the
      performer chooses the path, and the work is every path it allows — its futuribles. "Eleven cells" is written in
      Klavierstück XI's manner (not its notes): ten fragments, any of them first, each leaving how the next is played
      (tempo, dynamics, touch), the performance over when a fragment comes a third time; a few rules more — F never followed
      by F or G, H never by itself, after C only a rhythm at most six swaps from its own (the fragments' rhythms as necklaces
      of 16 pulses). Each fragment is a rhythm cell over four notes of one twelve-tone row; some begin alike (H and J share
      their pitches an octave apart). Followed: while a fragment sounds, every fragment that may come is a hypothesis,
      its opening aligned with what is heard (a wrong, missed or added note allowed); the one sure first is taken, and the path
      moves on by itself — the follower decides the form. Once the performance is over it is read again whole (the
      paths the rules allow, the readings at each point of the performance kept in a beam), and what was decided from the
      first notes only is corrected. Kept, the performances are a tree of branches: a beginning played twice is one
      branch. The work is a tree too, [:open … [:frag …] …], for the app's open box to come.
