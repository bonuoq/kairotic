# The studies

*[Leer en castellano](studies.es.md)* · [Documentation](README.md)

Kairotic was built as a series of **studies**: each one a question, answered by a working page
published as a claude.ai artifact, and kept as it was in [`studies/`](../studies). Studies 1–8 were
written in Spanish; from Study 9 on, code and interface are in English (the [glossary](glossary.md)
maps the old names to the new ones). From 0.1.0-alpha on, the app lives in [`app/`](../app) and grows
as one.

**Opening a study.** Kairotic Pages serves every study at its folder:
`https://bonuoq.github.io/kairotic/studies/<folder>/`, for example
[`studies/29-live/`](https://bonuoq.github.io/kairotic/studies/29-live/). There a study can use what a
page published on claude.ai cannot, such as the microphone. On your own computer, serve the folder and
open it on `localhost` (a page opened as a file cannot load its modules):

```sh
cd studies && python3 -m http.server 8000     # then http://localhost:8000/29-live/
```

| 37 | Notation | descriptors as a score (discussion #114): four lanes over time — **pitch** (MIDI), **loudness** (dB), **brightness** (the spectral centroid, Hz) and **noisiness** (the spectral flatness) — drawn with the pointer or read from a sound file as `features~` and `pitch~` hear it, and **realised by a `faust~`**: its `pitch` and `gain` sliders follow their lanes, its other sliders are chosen frame by frame from a bank of its own steady settings, heard, for the brightness and noisiness written (where a pitch is written, among the settings in which YIN would hear it); then it listens to what it played and asks for more where it fell short (three passes). Over four scores, a note of another instrument (the Faust twin of Study 30's synth) and the voice's own moving sliders, what sounds is off by 0.3 dB, 2.2 semitones of brightness, 0.014 of noisiness and about 10 cents on average, against 5.0 dB, 7.3 semitones and 0.022 for the voice held still at the mean of what is written. Not every score can be played: noisiness and brightness are tied in a voice (noise as dark as a tone does not exist in it), and the page shades the frames that ask for what the voice cannot do |
| 38 | Futuribles | the possible futures of a work beside its history (discussion #172): the **history** keeps only choices (yours or the engine's, with their branches); the **futuribles** are never stored, a pure function of a point of it and a **rule set** as data, and a choice is the only bridge between the two. First domain: a **canon** at a diatonic interval and a delay (the comes is the dux d notes later, shifted, so every rule between the voices falls on the dux against itself), first species after Fux: hard rules that make a note legal or not with a reason, soft ones that give it a cost — leaps are not penalised, only compensated by contrary motion (a 4th or a 5th turns back), and what costs is a line that stays still, hovers, runs one way or repeats itself mechanically, with the **memory of the work** (a figure used earlier costs less); **invertible counterpoint** as a transform of the voices checked by the same rules (at the 10th parallel 3rds become octaves, at the 12th a 6th becomes a 7th); **obligations** the next notes owe (turn back after a leap, end on the final). Hard rules look only at the last notes, so the ways to finish from any point are **counted exactly** (memoized on that window): their log₂ is the **divergence**, the bits of freedom left, drawn under the score, and the fan of futuribles shows each next note with its futures, the dead ends and why the others are not allowed; **why?** counts the futures again without each hard rule, to show which one closes a note or a point. The engine chooses only among notes with a future (never stuck), weighing each with the cheapest way the line can go on for the next notes (it **looks ahead**), with a seed and a **temperature** that can follow the divergence as in Copycat. A dorian canon at the octave below, two notes later, 12 notes: 15723 canons; in Scittle the engine writes one in about 30 ms, with 1% repeated notes and 38% leaps |
| 39 | Meter | dissonance and meter in Study 38's canon (discussion #172): a beat is strong when it starts a bar — **first species** a note per bar (every beat strong), **second species** two (2/2) or three (3/2), a note per beat. On a strong beat every vertical is a consonance; on a weak one a dissonance may pass if both voices come to it by step and leave it by step — a **passing** note or a **neighbour**, marked P / N under the score for the dux · the comes — and never two in a row; no 5ths or octaves on consecutive strong beats. The delay of the comes is in **beats**: an odd delay puts it on the other beats, so what is strong in the dux is weak in the comes. A dissonance that passes costs less. The hard rules and the exact count moved to a small **kernel in plain JavaScript** (`kernel.mjs`, its own tests in Node and in the browser), the rest stays ClojureScript: with twice the notes and rules that look further back Scittle took seconds; the kernel counts 496320 canons of 8 bars of 2/2, the comes 3 beats later, in about 0.15 s. Some canons have no futures: at the octave, one beat later, every step of the dux makes a 7th or a 9th on the next strong beat. And dissonance is rare (4 canons in 30 by the engine): in a canon both voices move on every beat, so a dissonance needs both to step at once — a voice that holds while the other moves (fourth species, syncopation) is the next step |
| 40 | Imitation | from a canon to free counterpoint (discussion #172): the comes is no longer derived from the dux but chosen note by note, and how much it imitates the dux is a **degree**: **1** a canon (hard), **0.9–0.5** near notes — within a 3rd of the dux's note, each step away costing more the nearer the degree is to 1 —, **0.4–0.2** only the motion — going the other way than the dux went costs —, below **0.2** free. You choose the dux; the engine chooses the comes (the cheapest by its own line, the verticals and the imitation) and you can change it, a branch like any choice; a diamond marks where a canon would put it. Both voices end together, as a canon breaks off into its cadence. The kernel counts the futures exactly over pairs of notes (at 1 the same count as Study 39); over 8 works by the engine of 12 notes of the comes, the notes off the canon are 0 at 1, about 3 at 0.7, about 9 at 0.3 and at 0 |
| 41 | Suspensions | ties and fourth species (discussions #172 and #192): in a voice the same pitch on the next beat is now the note **held** over the beat (a tie, two beats at most) — what a long syllable will need in aoidos (#192) —, and a dissonance may fall on a strong beat as a **suspension**: prepared by a consonance, held by one voice while the other moves, resolved a step down to a consonance; held above, a 7th, a 4th or a 9th (7-6, 4-3, 9-8), held below a 2nd (2-3), as Fux allows. The melodic rules and costs look at the notes struck. **4th: syncopation** is Fux's fourth species: the dux is the cantus firmus, a note a bar, and the comes is syncopated, holding its notes over the strong beats, where the suspensions fall; in a strict canon the comes is the cantus firmus half a bar later, breaking off into its cadence. Ties open Study 40's default canon from 442780 to 707134 canons; strict canons in fourth species are few (101 an octave below a beat later, 700 an octave above), and a degree of 0.8 opens them to 3611 |
| 42 | Systems | the system as data (discussions #172 and #192): Study 41's counterpoint on a **system** written in EDN — a **tuning** (its degrees in cents up to the period, as a Scala file lists them, and the Hz of the reference), the **steps** of a scale in it, the **quality** of each class of vertical (by its real size when that decides, as the perfect and the diminished 5th), the **melodic** moves allowed, the suspensions and cadences, and the **modes** (final, tenor, ambitus, starts). | 43 | Mirrors | canons by transformation (discussions #172 and #192): the comes is the dux **transformed**, the transformation as data — a canon, an **inversion** about an axis (contrary motion), an **augmentation** (twice as slow), a **crab** (backwards) and a **table canon** (backwards and upside down: the one score read from across the table, a musical ambigram). | 44 | Ring | a circular buffer of messages in **time** for a delay that changes while it plays (after #176): it keeps the last seconds from its first message (or from `:clear`) and forgets what is older — circular in time, not in a count of messages (`xf/partition n 1` is that). | 45 | Ring~ | Study 44's ring for **sound**: a circular buffer of audio (an AudioWorklet) that keeps the last seconds, the write head going round it at the speed of time and the read head d behind. | 47 | Heads | canons and rings as one model (discussion "Heads on a span of time"): a **span** of time — circular (a ring) or fixed (a work) — and **heads** that write and read it, each a head of its own, as a `reader` is a box of its own. Where a head is comes from a **clock** (a start and a speed), a **signal** (a clock moved by a slow wave), a **hand**, or, for a reader, **following** a writer: first its distance d, then its speed.
